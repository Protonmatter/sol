Earth layers

Imagery keeps its source date as the model clock moves. Antarctica and Greenland appear in the land map. Sea ice is a dated scientific color overlay; cloud imagery has gaps. The blue atmospheric rim is an illustrative scattering effect, not a measurement of current air conditions.

Overlays

A moon passing between the Sun and its planet drops a real shadow on the sunlit face, and one passing behind is darkened by the planet's. Both are computed from the moon's true distance, so a shadow will not sit under its marker: by default the marker rides a widened orbit that lifts it clear of the enlarged disc while the shadow lands where it really falls; in True scale the small residue is Sun-versus-viewer parallax. Shadow timing shares the moon layer's view-grade accuracy - not an eclipse ephemeris.

Sources & limits for all three views

Sol is for research and learning, not operational forecasting, navigation or safety decisions. SDO browse imagery is observed; magnetic model regions and cycle frames are synthetic. Without capture-time registration, those views stay separate.

Sky calculations stay on your device by default. A configured external recipient requires explicit consent before receiving location and time. Accuracy depends on the body, quantity, epoch and reference evidence; no blanket arcsecond accuracy or full accessibility conformance is asserted.

Inspect the selected view's source and time, then open its model/data disclosure for raw evidence. Snapshot export is a scientific record, not a resumable transport checkpoint.
