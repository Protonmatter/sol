## Reproduced planet failure

On native Qualcomm Adreno X1-85 through ANGLE/D3D11, selecting Mars and immediately
selecting Jupiter while the three physical programs were loading made Jupiter's
illustrative texture become `unavailable`. The browser emitted repeated
`GL_INVALID_VALUE: glGetProgramiv: Program object expected` warnings after pending
shader cancellation. A fresh Jupiter view without that transition rendered its
texture correctly.

Cancellation deleted unfinished linked programs. The resulting delayed driver
errors reached the next texture upload's error check, which correctly rejected the
upload. The fix retains and reuses unfinished GPU allocations while cancelling the
public request and stopping polling immediately. It does not clear error flags to
hide upload failures. Completed unused jobs are reclaimed on subsequent requests;
active and retired jobs share a ceiling of 32 managed programs per context. Context
loss invalidates the pool. Explicit retry and the 30-second deadline are preserved.

The corrected native run exercised all three pending physical programs, then
verified Jupiter's texture and eight camera angles each for Mars and Jupiter.
Mars's optional physical pass reached its deadline before its angle sweep. Both
planet textures remained ready, every sampled GL error was zero, and no graphics
warnings or page errors occurred. An exploratory sweep also checked eight angles
per planet under both illustrative and visible-light Sun modes (34 captures).

The original angle-specific Mars disappearance was not independently reproduced.
Darkening on the night side was present with the texture still visible; lighting
semantics were not changed. The optional Mars physical-atmosphere programs still
time out on this GPU. This change prevents that failure from damaging the surface
maps and other planets; it does not qualify the physical pass on this device.

## Corona clearance and disabled layers

The two subsequent comments on `b02f844` were reproduced before modification.
The display solver used a 1.35-radius Sun envelope while the approved look drew
out to 2.1 radii. In the held 0.307 AU Mercury fixture this allowed overlapping
displayed volumes. The solver now uses the selected envelope and includes it in
the cache key, so paused mode/layer toggles cannot reuse incompatible radii.
Physical-scale radii and physical centers remain unchanged. The camera-fit test
compares each mode against its actually submitted Sun radius, since enlarged
display radii can now differ between modes.

The second regression showed that turning Texture layers off was described as a
distance deferral. The shared Sun description now states that the layer is off.
Tests cover the status panel, Sun controls and destination card, re-enabling the
layer, and genuine distant deferral.

Validation passed 1,374 Node tests, 409 Python tests, both native and software
browser suites, staged smoke and 97.46% combined runtime line coverage. A native
Adreno/D3D11 probe read actual submitted radii at a held real ephemeris: the approved
envelope sum was 0.44334144 AU against a 0.44334143 AU clearance limit, within
Float32 readback tolerance. It verified mode/layer restoration and unchanged epoch
and physical positions, with no graphics warnings or errors. This is a 3-D volume
clearance guarantee; it does not forbid projected planetary transits.
